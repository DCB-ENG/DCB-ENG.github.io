Simple torque–tension estimate using nominal thread stress area and assumed friction. Correct tightening can require a much more specific procedure.

Calculation method & important limits

Torque (N·m) = K × preload (N) × nominal diameter (mm) ÷ 1,000

The preload target is 75% of proof stress × metric tensile stress area, where stress area = π/4 × (diameter − 0.938194 × pitch)². Steel property classes use typical proof-stress values from ISO 898-1; stainless classes use approximate minimum 0.2% proof stress values from ISO 3506-1. These strength standards do not prescribe a universal tightening torque.

  • The calculator deliberately withholds general estimates above M39, and class 9.8 above M16. Stainless class 80 also requires manufacturer data above M24. M50 and some other sizes/pitches are non-preferred: verify actual hardware designation.
  • Dry and lubricated are generic assumptions, not certified friction coefficients. Oil, anti-seize, plating, washers, surface finish and repeated tightening affect preload. Stainless fasteners are especially susceptible to galling.
  • Verify the nut grade, threaded engagement, mating material, bearing face and joint design. These values do not establish suitability for aluminium threads or tapped materials.
  • For structural/preloaded connections, lifting, automotive wheels, engines, pressure equipment and safety-critical work, use the applicable engineer's or manufacturer's approved torque/tension procedure.

References: ISO 898-1 (carbon/alloy steel fastener strength), ISO 3506-1 (corrosion-resistant steel strength) and metric thread dimensions. Values are for estimating and reference only.
